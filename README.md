# BAA 16.0 — Final Presentation & Future of Agentic AI
### General Assembly · Build AI Agents · Capstone Module

---

## Overview

This is the capstone lab for General Assembly's **Build AI Agents (BAA)** course. It synthesizes all 15 prior modules into a single interactive experience — culminating in a final agent demonstration, business impact evaluation, risk assessment, and a forward-looking exploration of where Agentic AI is heading.

Students complete this lab after building, securing, and monitoring a production-ready agent prototype (ContentPilot) across Modules 2–15.

**Live URL:** `https://raahimaahmed-png.github.io/Lab16_Final_Presentation_Future/`

---

## Learning Objectives

By completing this lab, students will be able to:

- Synthesize how all 15 BAA modules connect into a unified agent-building framework
- Deliver a structured 4-minute agent demonstration pitch to a business audience
- Evaluate an agent's business value across ROI, scalability, time-to-value, risk, and differentiation
- Map an agent's key risks on a likelihood × impact matrix and articulate mitigation strategies
- Assess the near- and long-term trajectory of Agentic AI across six emerging developments
- Articulate a defensible point of view on autonomous systems, governance, and human oversight

## Section Details

### 🗺 Course Synthesis — How It All Connects

Five collapsible phase blocks map every module to the agent-building lifecycle:

- **Phase 1 · Understand** — Labs 01–03: What agents are and how they work
- **Phase 2 · Choose & Build** — Labs 04, 06, 07: Frameworks, prompts, and tool integration
- **Phase 3 · Specialize** — Labs 05, 08, 09, 12: Memory, dialogue, and case analysis
- **Phase 4 · Secure & Ship** — Labs 10, 11, 13: Security, automation, and ethics
- **Phase 5 · Operate & Evolve** — Labs 14, 15: Build, monitor, and iterate at scale

Each lab entry shows its core concept and how it connects forward to the next phase. A visual synthesis ring displays the eight competencies (System Prompt, Agent Loop, Tool Calling, Memory, Dialogue Design, Security, Evaluation, Monitoring) that constitute a production agent.

### 🎤 Final Presentation Builder

A structured 5-section pitch form producing a live slide preview:

| Section | Time Budget | What to Include |
|---|---|---|
| Agent Name & Purpose | 30 sec | Name + one-line value statement |
| Workflow Walkthrough | 90 sec | 3-step pipeline: input → reasoning → output |

## Instructor Notes

**Time allocation:** The lab is designed for a 90-minute session. The Presentation Builder and Futures Explorer sections work well as breakout activities — students can share their slide previews and discussion responses with the group.

**Facilitation tip for Futures Explorer:** The discussion questions are designed to surface disagreement. Questions about liability distribution (Lab 05), human oversight in self-improving agents (Lab 06), and persistent memory governance (Lab 04) reliably generate divergent responses. Give students 2–3 minutes to answer individually before discussing as a group — the contrast between responses is the learning moment.

**The Course Map** in Section 01 works as a standalone review tool. Students who struggled with earlier modules can use it to identify gaps before the final presentation — each phase entry shows exactly which concept belongs to which module.
